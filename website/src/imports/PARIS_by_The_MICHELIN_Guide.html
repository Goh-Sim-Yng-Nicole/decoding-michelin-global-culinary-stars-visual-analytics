A Foodie Guide to Belleville in Paris

Belleville, often hailed as one of Paris' trendiest neighborhoods, actually remains deeply rooted in its heritage. This eclectic pocket of the city blends a rich history, from its days of socialist anarchism, with a vibrant mix of cultures. Its unpolished streets are alive with authentic global flavors, making Belleville a must-visit destination for food-lovers.

Visit Paris

Welcome to Paris, the City of Light, which we invite you to discover in all its glory with Paris by The MICHELIN Guide! Showcasing the 'best of the best' among Paris's restaurants and hotels, this Guide includes daily recommendations from our experts. From all the central neighborhoods to the suburbs of Greater Paris, our selection is constantly updated with the latest news.

Discover the openings that everyone is talking about: Paris's best bistros and brasseries, MICHELIN-Starred restaurants that are among the cheapest in France and Bib Gourmands offering amazing value for money. And that's not all: day after day, we also recommend here the best bakeries, brunch spots, cocktail bars, spas, and more — allowing you to experience Paris like a real Parisian. On the terraces of legendary Parisian cafés and tea rooms, enjoy croissants, chocolate éclairs, Paris-Brest, tarte Tatin, macarons, and other Parisian sweet treats.

Our specialist hotel experts also compile the best hotels in Paris. From luxury hotels to affordable hotels, including the best located hotels in Paris and those with the most beautiful views, we have everything you need to plan a magical trip to Paris, and to make the most of the 2024 Olympic Games!

Where to go out in Paris? With us, you can explore all the districts one by one, discovering major historical and cultural highlights as you go: the 1st arrondissement, with the iconic Louvre museum and the Jardin des Tuileries; Le Marais, straddling the 3rd and 4th arrondissements; the 6th arrondissement, dominated by Saint-Germain-des-Prés, home of the existentialist movement of the 1940s and 1950s; The Golden Triangle, the center of Parisian luxury, with its countless haute couture houses, luxury boutiques and palaces; the 9th arrondissement, from Butte Montmartre to the Palais Garnier, via SoPi, Printemps and the Galeries Lafayette, along with shopping arcades like Passage Jouffroy and Passage Verdeau; and last but not least, the best trendy restaurants and bars in the 11th arrondissement, across districts like Oberkampf and Bastille.

Our Inspectors search Paris with a fine-tooth comb, to exclusively unearth the city's best restaurants and hotels, as well as its best-kept secrets — from hidden terraces and discreet rooftop bars to delicatessens and Parisian markets popular with the greatest chefs. Across the Inspectors' many recommendations, our Paris Travel Guide provides you with the perfect opportunity to taste local specialties against a backdrop of good French wines.

As we're sure you know by now, this Guide is specially designed to help you have a perfect stay in Paris, to optimize your trip and to guarantee you a trouble-free experience. For a full-on holiday or just a long weekend, The MICHELIN Guide will help you (re)discover the best places in Paris.

Frequently asked questions

How many Starred restaurants are there in Paris?

In The MICHELIN Guide 2024, Paris stands out for having no less than 121 Starred restaurants – a testament to its exceptional gastronomic wealth.

Is Paris the city with the most Michelin Stars in the world?

The city of Paris ranks second in the world for Starred restaurants, just behind Tokyo in Japan.

What are the affordable restaurants recommended by The MICHELIN Guide in Paris?

Contrary to popular belief, not all restaurants recommended by The MICHELIN Guide are exorbitantly expensive 'fine dining' establishments. From trendy bistros and luxury restaurants to village pubs and street food, The MICHELIN Guide has something to satisfy all tastes, occasions and budgets – most notably with its Bib Gourmand award, for restaurants that offer good value for money. You can also find a list of the cheapest Michelin-Starred restaurants in Paris on our website.

When is the best time to book a hotel in Paris?

Peak Season: May to September constitutes peak season in Paris, especially during the summer months. Advance booking is recommended during this time because demand is high, particularly for well-located hotels. Off Season: November to February is considered off season in Paris, except during Christmas and New Year. Rates may be more affordable in these months, and you have a better chance of finding last-minute deals.
